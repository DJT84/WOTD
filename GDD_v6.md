# Way of the Dodo — Game Design Document v6

*[Game title TBC. Working title: Way of the Dodo.]*

Solo dev. Godot 4. Steam.

---

## What kind of game this is

An incremental game about cosmic destruction.

You are an apprentice. Your Master is ancient, cosmic, and gleefully professional about extinction. Earth is your training exercise. The Master has selected it personally. It is, apparently, an insignificant rock. The Master takes inspiration from game Dungeon Keeper.

The heart of it is two numbers that grow.

- **Cosmic Dust** — your currency. Generated by impacts and big events. Spent on prestige upgrades between epochs. Never reset mid-run.
- **Cosmic Energy** — your progress bar. Generated exclusively by big events: earthquakes, eruptions, chain reactions, extinctions, acid rain, vents. Fills the epoch energy bar. When full, you have enough power to fire the epoch-ending asteroid. Regular impacts do not generate it — only significant destruction does.

Everything feeds one or both of these numbers. The game is managing both simultaneously while the Master offers its opinions on your technique.

### The floor and the ceiling

**Floor — everyone advances.** Every player generates enough Cosmic Energy to fire the epoch asteroid eventually. No one gets stuck. The bar fills faster with active, skilled play — slower with idle play — but it always fills.

**Ceiling — how well did you do it?** Total Cosmic Dust accumulated across the run is your lifetime score. A skilled player who hunts Big Game, triggers chain reactions, manages their belt, and collects debris will accumulate significantly more Dust than a casual player. Same epochs. Not the same score.

## Tone — The Master

The governing voice of the game is **The Master** — ancient, cosmic, and not entirely hiding how much it enjoys this.

The Master is not evil. Evil implies a grudge. The Master simply finds extinction *satisfying* in the way a craftsperson finds good work satisfying. It has standards. It has opinions. It has been doing this since before Earth had an atmosphere and it finds that detail mildly amusing.

You are the apprentice. Earth is the assignment. The Master has seen a billion worlds. This one barely registers — and yet here it is, invested in whether you do it properly.

The tone sits between Dungeon Keeper's gleeful malevolence and a senior colleague who pretends not to care how you're doing while watching very carefully. The relish is always there underneath the professionalism. When you do something spectacular the Master's composure slips slightly. It doesn't comment immediately. It waits a beat.

*"...note the propagation pattern. Good."*

### When the Master speaks

The Master does not narrate constantly. It speaks at key moments:

- **Game opening** — sets up the premise, introduces Earth, introduces you
- **Each epoch introduction** — names the asteroid, places it in context, expresses mild anticipation
- **Each age objective** — brief instruction, deadpan, slightly condescending
- **Fragment collection** — acknowledges the find, reveals coordinates, never effusive
- **First time triggering a new event type** — notes it. May express approval. Does not explain it.
- **Missed debris** — disappointed. Briefly.
- **Big Game Hunter kill** — satisfaction, controlled
- **Big Game Hunter miss** — silence, then one line
- **Epoch asteroid launch** — the Master's best lines live here
- **The ending** — *[TBC — ending under review]*


### The apprentice

The player is an apprentice cosmic destroyer. This is not laboured. It is established in one line of the opening and implied through everything that follows. The player does not have dialogue. They act. The Master comments - maybe praise, or critical in joking way.

Earth being a training exercise is background context — it explains the Master's tone without demanding the player engage with it consciously.

## Structure

**8 epochs × 3 ages = 24 gates.**

Each epoch covers a distinct period of Earth's history. Each epoch has three ages — Early, Middle, Late — representing increasing difficulty within that period.

Target playtime: approximately 2.5 hours minimum. Roughly 4 minutes of active play per gate, plus upgrade decisions between epochs.

| # | Epoch | Life |
|---|-------|------|
| 1 | Cambrian | Strange ocean creatures, hydrothermal vent life |
| 2 | Carboniferous | Giant insects, swamp life, coal forest creatures |
| 3 | Permian | Synapsids, early reptiles, Pangaea megafauna |
| 4 | Jurassic | Early dinosaurs, marine reptiles |
| 5 | Cretaceous | T-Rex and late dinosaurs |
| 6 | Eocene | Early mammals, warm greenhouse megafauna |
| 7 | Pleistocene | Mammoths, ice age megafauna |
| 8 | Holocene | Modern world — hardest epoch |

## The core loop

**Within each epoch:**

1. Hit planet → triggers events → generates Cosmic Dust (always) and Cosmic Energy (events only)
2. Scan planet with cursor → find and tag high value targets
3. Fire special belt asteroids at tagged targets → chain reactions, Big Game kills, extinctions
4. Collect debris from impacts — three types (see Debris section)
5. Complete age objectives → collect epoch asteroid fragments → coordinates resolve
6. Cosmic Energy bar fills → epoch asteroid assembled → launch at confirmed target
7. Epoch-ending impact event fires → epoch closes
8. Upgrade screen → spend accumulated Cosmic Dust on prestige upgrades → next epoch

**The two parallel hunts running simultaneously:**
- Debris hunt (objectives, gain epoch lasting upgrades and targetable asteroids) — closes the epoch
- High value target hunt (active, skill-based) — improves the run

## The escalation

Eight epochs of Earth's history. Each epoch is a big jump in scale. Early on you wipe out a few thousand tiny ocean creatures. By the end you are ending billions. The numbers get huge. Reaching a new epoch should feel like a step up into something vast. The Master notices. Briefly.

## Cosmic Dust and Cosmic Energy

### Cosmic Dust

Generated by: regular meteor impacts AND big events.
Used for: prestige upgrade purchases at epoch end.
Accumulated freely throughout the run, spent between epochs.
Your lifetime score is total Cosmic Dust accumulated across a full run.

### Cosmic Energy

Generated by: **big events only.**

| Event | Generates Cosmic Energy |
|-------|------------------------|
| Earthquake | Yes |
| Volcanic eruption | Yes |
| Supervolcano | Yes — large amount |
| Acid rain | Yes |
| Wildfire | Yes |
| Tsunami | Yes |
| Hydrothermal plume | Yes |
| Ocean acidification | Yes |
| Plague | Yes |
| Methane release | Yes |
| Species extinction | Yes — scales with species significance |
| Chain reaction (any) | Yes — per reaction in chain |
| Regular meteor impact | **No** |
| Bombardment hit | **No** |

Each event type has a defined Cosmic Energy value in resource data. Bigger, rarer events generate more. The Cosmic Energy bar fills through sustained active destruction — the player who triggers chains, hunts species, and hits high-value terrain fills it significantly faster than the player who idles.

**Everyone reaches the threshold eventually. Skilled play reaches it faster and with more Cosmic Dust accumulated.**

## The Epoch Asteroid System

Each epoch has a named asteroid and two target coordinates on Earth. Finding the asteroid fragments and confirming the coordinates is the epoch's meta-objective. Firing the assembled asteroid at the confirmed target is what closes the epoch.

### Fragment structure per epoch

**Early age:** Complete the age objective → collect 1 fragment → reveals **latitude** of target.

**Middle age:** Complete the age objective → collect 1 fragment → reveals **longitude** of target. Target is now fully confirmed. The player knows exactly where they are aiming.

**Late age:** Hunt 2–5 additional fragments (varies per epoch, defined in epoch resource data) through debris collection and triggered events. When all fragments are collected, the asteroid is assembled and can be fired.

**4 fragments total for single-target epochs. Eocene is the exception — two confirmed real-world targets, Popigai and Chesapeake Bay — Early age reveals coordinates for Popigai, Middle age reveals coordinates for Chesapeake Bay, Late age assembles and fires both in sequence.**

### Fragment drops

- Percentage chance drops from debris collection and specific triggered events. Defined per epoch in resource data. Miss one and the same triggering condition can drop it again at the same percentage. You cannot be permanently locked out, only delayed.

### The coordinate reveal UI

A persistent small globe or crosshair display updates as fragments are collected:

- Latitude confirmed → horizontal line locks onto the globe
- Longitude confirmed → vertical line locks. Crosshair snaps to the target location. It pulses.

From this moment the player knows where the epoch ends. They enter the Late age with a target and a partially assembled asteroid.

### Fragment collection

fragments are collected through the debris system — they appear as a distinct debris type in orbit (visually unique — heavier, darker, fragment-shaped rather than the standard debris, they have a glow). Cursor hover collects them. They do not auto-apply like upgrade pickups — they load into the asteroid assembly tracker.

Some epochs also have specific triggered event drops — defined in epoch resource data. For example, a supervolcano event in the Permian has a very high percent or 100% percentage chance of ejecting a fragment, you just need to find the supervolcanoe, for example..

### Firing the epoch asteroid

When the Cosmic Energy bar is full AND all Late age fragments are collected, the epoch asteroid sits assembled in a dedicated launch slot in the belt — distinct from standard and special weapon slots. It is the largest object in the belt. It glows.

The player drags it onto the confirmed target location on the planet. It always hits. The epoch impact event fires. It is spectaclular, order of degrees larger than regular asteroids.

If the player has the energy but not the fragments, the asteroid sits inert. If they have the fragments but not the energy, same. Both conditions must be met.

**The asteroid cannot be fired early.** If dragged before both conditions are met, it returns to the belt. One line from the Master:

*"Not yet. Do it properly."*

## The Cursor

The cursor is the player's primary tool for everything outside direct asteroid throwing. It does two distinct jobs depending on where it is positioned, switching automatically.

### In space (outside the atmosphere ring)

- Appears as a **glowing collection circle**
- Size is upgradeable — larger circle catches more debris
- **Automatically collects debris by hovering** — no clicking required
- Player positions it anticipating debris arcs from recent impacts
- Skilled players keep it in the debris stream while scanning for next target

### Over the planet (inside the atmosphere ring)

- Cursor changes visually — different colour, tighter shape, magnifying glass quality
- Acts as a scanner — **reveals hidden high value targets on tiles beneath it**
- Without the cursor over a tile, high value targets are not visible
- Larger scope radius means multiple targets can be revealed and tagged simultaneously with a single click — lucky overlaps reward upgraded scope investment
- **Click to place a tag/pin on a revealed target**

### The sacrifice

The cursor cannot do both jobs at once. It is either in space collecting debris, or on the planet scanning for targets. Every positioning decision is a micro-trade-off:

- Debris arc incoming → move to space to catch it
- High value tile coming around → move to planet to scan and tag it
- Big Game Hunter window opening → tag before it rotates away or catch the debris from your last shot?

This constant low-level cursor management is the active engagement layer of the game. It never demands attention. It always rewards it.

## Tags

Tags mark high value targets for special weapon deployment. They persist through planet rotation — the tagged tile disappears around the back of the planet and the tag remains, ready for when it returns to view.

**Tag types, each a distinct colour:**

| Target Type | Colour | Notes |
|-------------|--------|-------|
| Fault line | — | Geological, earthquake chain potential |
| Volcano | — | Guaranteed eruption on direct hit |
| Hydrothermal vent | — | Plume event, ocean tile damage |
| Big Game Hunter target | — | Timed window — tag before window closes |
| Deep sea vent cluster | — | High Cosmic Energy on destruction |

*[Colours to be defined in art pass.]*

**Tag capacity starts at 2, upgradeable.** More tags means more pre-planned shots queued up as targets rotate into view. A fully upgraded tag system with a large scope lets the player build a priority hit list that covers most of the visible planet.

Tags persist until used or manually removed. No time limit.

## The Asteroid Belt

The player's loadout. An arc of rocks displayed beneath the planet, always visible, always ready. Not a UI bar — a primary visual object alongside the planet. The belt communicates the player's full arsenal at a glance.

### Slot types

**Standard slots** — bread and butter asteroids. Drag, throw, impact, cooldown, repeat. Start with 3 slots, upgradeable. Independent cooldowns.

Standard asteroid tiers named after real impacts in ascending size and power:
**Chelyabinsk → Barringer → Tunguska → Chicxulub → Vredefort**

Each tier is a meaningful visual and mechanical step up. The Chicxulub asteroid looks categorically different to the Chelyabinsk — bigger silhouette, different colour treatment, heavier screen shake on impact.

**Special weapon slots** — special weapons collected from debris. Visually distinct from standard rocks: different silhouette, colour, particle glow. Start with 1 slot, upgradeable.

**Epoch asteroid slot** — single dedicated slot for the assembled epoch-ending asteroid. Separate from standard and special weapon slots. Only populated in the Late age once all fragments are collected. Largest object in the belt. Always glowing.

### Belt management

Cooldown is tuned so good targets appear faster than standard slots recharge. The player can never hit everything and must always choose. The epoch asteroid adds a third layer — it sits there ready but cannot fire until the Cosmic Energy bar is full. The player sees it waiting.

## Debris — three types

Large aimed strikes and major bombardment events eject debris into orbit. The cursor collects it by hovering in space.

**Three debris types, visually distinct:**

| Type | Visual | Collection | Effect |
|------|--------|-----------|--------|
| Standard rocks | Dark, tumbling, no glow | Auto on hover | Loads into available standard belt slot. Replenishes ammunition. |
| Special asteroid fragments | Glowing, distinctive silhouette per type | Auto on hover | Loads directly into special weapon slot as a specific weapon |
| Upgrade pickups | Bright, geometrically distinct, different particle effect | Auto on hover | Applies instantly to current run. Master notification fires. |
| Epoch asteroid fragments | Heavy, dark, angular — clearly a piece of something larger | Auto on hover | Loads into epoch asteroid assembly tracker |

**Four types total.** All collected by cursor hover. Visually distinct enough that the player knows what they are chasing before they reach it.

### Debris sources

**Aimed strikes** — significant impacts eject debris. The type depends on what caused it:

| Impact Type | Debris Produced |
|-------------|----------------|
| Massive direct impact | Special weapon fragment or upgrade pickup |
| Volcano / fault chain reaction | Special geological weapon fragment |
| Ocean / coastal impact | Special ocean weapon fragment |
| Big Game Hunter kill | Special weapon fragment (heat seeker type) |
| Supervolcano | Upgrade pickup (guaranteed) |
| Late age — any significant impact | Percentage chance of epoch asteroid fragment |

**Bombardment** — does not produce debris from regular hits. Exception: if bombardment triggers a major chain reaction — supervolcano, massive earthquake cascade — that event ejects debris. Rare. Feels like the universe doing something on your behalf by accident. Bombardment prestige upgrades increase the chance of this happening.

### Debris trajectory

Debris flies from the impact site along a visible arc toward the screen edge. The player can track it from the moment of impact. Faint trail lingers briefly at the edge if missed. The Master noticed.

## Special weapons

Seven special weapon types, each a distinct belt item. All bypass terrain requirements — they fire regardless of what's on the target tile.

| Weapon | Effect | Best Used On |
|--------|--------|-------------|
| Guaranteed Earthquake | Full earthquake chain, maximum propagation | Fault line tiles — chain runs the full length |
| Guaranteed Eruption | Volcanic eruption chain, full lava spread | Any tile — creates lava spread to neighbours |
| Submarine Vent Trigger | Superheated plume across all surrounding ocean tiles | Ocean clusters, coastal life |
| Tsunami Strike | Wave sweep across all coastal tiles in arc | Coastal and shallow water life |
| Acid Rain Canister | Acid rain at maximum radius immediately | Dense land clusters |
| Big Game Heat Seeker | Seeks and strikes the nearest tagged Big Game Hunter target | Save for active hunt windows |
| Species Destroyer | Sets target species population to 0 on all tiles simultaneously | High-population non-Big-Game species |

## Targeting decisions

**Four simultaneous decisions on every aimed strike:**

1. Which tile to hit (terrain, life density, chain reaction potential)
2. Which asteroid to use (standard tier vs. special weapon vs. epoch asteroid)
3. Whether to wait for a tagged target to rotate into view
4. Where to position the cursor — collect debris from last shot or scan for next target

| Fault line adjacent | +20% earthquake chance | Earthquake | Linear along fault |
| Coastal shallow | Tsunami damage amplified | Wave sweep | Linear toward land |
| Deep ocean | Tsunami passes through | None | None |
| Deep sea vent cluster | High Cosmic Energy on destruction | Plume cascade | Wide ocean radius |

## Upgrades — prestige tree

Dust accumulates throughout each epoch. The upgrade screen opens between epochs. Dust is spent on the prestige upgrade tree. Upgrades are permanent across epochs within a run.

### Five upgrade branches

**Bombardment**
- Faster — more rocks per second
- Heavier — more Dust and Cosmic Energy chance per rock
- Wider — larger blast radius

**Asteroids**
- Bigger rock — tier upgrades (Chelyabinsk through Vredefort)
- More standard slots (starts at 3)
- More special weapon slots (starts at 1)
- Shorter cooldown

**Geological**
- Earthquake chance and propagation
- Volcano eruption trigger chance and lava spread
- Fault line sensitivity
- Supervolcano unlock (requires Volcano investment)

**Atmospheric**
- Acid rain chance and duration
- Impact winter reach and duration
- Wildfire spread rate

**Biological**
- Ocean acidification potency
- Plague spread rate
- Methane release chance

**Cursor and scope**
- Scope radius (reveals more tiles simultaneously)
- Collection circle size (catches more debris per pass)
- Tag capacity (more simultaneous tags)
- Tag persistence bonus

### Upgrade cost scaling

Exponential within each branch. Base cost × scaling factor per level. Example at 2.5× scaling:

| Level | Cost |
|-------|------|
| 1 | 100 |
| 2 | 250 |
| 3 | 625 |
| 4 | 1,562 |
| 5 | 3,906 |

Tuning target: Run 1 — first 2–3 levels of chosen branches. Run 2 — reaches 4–5 in primary branch. Run 3+ — approaches but never trivially reaches the top of any branch.

### In-run upgrade pickups

Upgrade pickups collected from debris apply instantly to the current run. The Master's notification fires. These are distinct from prestige upgrades — they are temporary, run-specific boosts that complement the permanent tree.

Examples of pickup types: temporary bombardment rate boost, one-time scope enlargement, bonus tag slot for the current epoch, Cosmic Dust multiplier for next chain reaction.

## Big Game Hunter

Each epoch has a Big Game Hunter list — five target species drawn from Early and Middle age species. The full list is displayed throughout the epoch. Only current-age targets are highlighted; others greyed.

Each highlighted target must be exterminated within a timed window per age. Missing the window means the hunt bonus is lost. The age advances regardless.

**Hunt rewards:** bonus Cosmic Dust, bonus Cosmic Energy, guaranteed debris ejection on successful kill.

**Upgrade tree reveals target information progressively:**
- Base: five targets listed by name, no location
- Upgrade 1: tile terrain preference revealed
- Upgrade 2: subtle visual pulse when target tile is visible
- Upgrade 3: persistent marker on target tiles

## Extinctions

When a species' total population reaches 0:

- A free standard asteroid spawns immediately (bypasses slots, no cooldown) — size scales with species significance
- Extinction stamp flashes centre screen: 2D illustration, species name, flavour text. Skippable.
- Species removed from active list
- Cosmic Energy awarded — scales with species significance
- When the stage extinction threshold is met, the next age begins

### Animals in space

On massive impacts, supervolcano events, or Big Game Hunter kills, a 2D sprite of the affected species tumbles slowly out of frame. Rare — reserved for significant moments. Each species has one exit appropriate to its dignity:

- Mammoth — tumbles slowly, stoic
- T-Rex — has the arms
- Anomalocaris — spins like a confused frisbee
- Dodo — flaps once, unconvincingly

### Miss stamp

When a Big Game Hunter window closes without a kill:

- Same format as extinction stamp, distinct visual treatment
- Master's voice: *"It got away."* *[beat]* *"We don't talk about that."*

## Prestige — Heat Death

After completing all eight epochs and the ending, the player is offered:

*"The universe has reached maximum entropy. Begin again?"*

Confirm: *"Obviously."*
Cancel: *"Not yet."*

On prestige:
- Cosmic Dust resets to zero
- Epoch asteroid fragments reset
- All upgrade levels reset
- **Prestige upgrades persist** — a separate permanent layer above the run upgrade tree, purchased with a prestige-only currency accumulated across runs
- Entropy (lifetime score) never resets — always climbing across all runs

**Style-linked prestige reward** — the game analyses how the player played: which damage types they triggered most, which upgrades they invested in, how many Big Game kills they made, how much debris they collected. The prestige reward is weighted toward their demonstrated style. A geological player gets better geological prestige upgrades. The Master observes the tendency. Does not comment directly. Adjusts the curriculum.

## The planet — technical specification

### Architecture: 3D Geodesic Sphere in Godot 4

The planet is a **3D geodesic sphere** built from a subdivided icosphere. This is the core visual object of the game.

**Do not use a cylinder. Do not fake the sphere in 2D. The full circle of the planet must be visible.**

#### Geodesic sphere generation

1. Start with a regular icosahedron (20 triangular faces, 12 vertices)
2. Subdivide each triangular face N times (N=4 or N=5)
3. Project all vertices onto the unit sphere
4. Dual the mesh: convert triangular faces to hexagonal tiles with exactly 12 pentagonal tiles
5. Each resulting polygon is one surface tile

**Recommended: N=4 (162 tiles).**

#### Tile data structure

```
tile_id: int
center_position: Vector3
normal: Vector3
neighbours: Array[int]
is_pentagon: bool
terrain_type: String            # "ocean_deep", "ocean_shallow", "land", "coast", "ice", "permafrost"
landmark_type: String           # "" = none, "mountain", "volcano", "vent", "fault", "glacier", "city"
landmark_supports_life: bool
is_volcano: bool
is_fault_adjacent: bool
is_deep_sea_vent: bool
damage_state: int               # 0=healthy, 1=damaged, 2=scorched
life_count: float               # 0.0–1.0
life_suppressed: bool
suppression_timer: float
tag_type: String                # "" = untagged, or tag type if player has tagged
```

#### Tile mesh and assets

Tile meshes generated procedurally in GDScript. ShaderMaterial handles: base colour, atmosphere rim colour, edge darkening, damage state interpolation, life density warmth shift.

Planet rotates continuously on Y-axis. Approximately 1 full rotation per 90 seconds. Never stops.

#### Life on tiles

Life is data only. No visual clusters on the planet surface. Represented through: hover panel, extinction stamp, statistics screen.

Regrowth: if a tile is not suppressed and not being hit, `life_count` slowly increases toward 1.0. Rate tunable per epoch.

#### Landmarks

3D meshes on tile surfaces. Position at tile centre, orient along tile normal. Scale exaggerated — must read clearly from camera distance.

**Landmark life rules:**

| Landmark | Supports Life | Notes |
|----------|--------------|-------|
| Volcano | No | life_count = 0 |
| Hydrothermal vent | No | life_count = 0 |
| Fault line | No | life_count = 0 |

## Aimed strike — implementation

### Impact resolution

1. Calculate Cosmic Dust gain: `base_dust * life_count * epoch_multiplier * asteroid_size_multiplier`
2. Calculate Cosmic Energy gain: **0 for regular impact.** Only events generate Cosmic Energy.
3. Reduce `life_count` on hit tile and neighbours
4. Update `damage_state`
5. Play impact animation and screen shake
6. Check terrain type — apply terrain bonuses
7. If special weapon: apply effect before chain reaction roll
8. Roll for chain reactions — each triggered reaction generates Cosmic Energy
9. Check extinction conditions — extinction generates Cosmic Energy
10. Check debris ejection — aimed strikes on significant impacts eject debris
11. Slot cooldown starts / special weapon slot becomes EMPTY

### Special weapon resolution

Special weapons resolve before the chain reaction roll:
- **Guaranteed Earthquake:** Sets earthquake as triggered, maximum propagation
- **Guaranteed Eruption:** Sets volcanic eruption triggered, full lava spread
- **Submarine Vent Trigger:** Plume on all ocean tiles in radius regardless of terrain
- **Tsunami Strike:** Wave sweep across coastal arc
- **Acid Rain Canister:** Acid rain at maximum radius immediately
- **Big Game Heat Seeker:** Calculates nearest tagged Big Game target, teleports impact there
- **Species Destroyer:** Sets target species population to 0 on all tiles, triggers extinction immediately

---

## Chain Reactions

On any impact:

1. Check terrain for guaranteed triggers (volcano = guaranteed eruption)
2. Roll remaining damage types against current chances
3. Apply area of effect for triggered type — generate Cosmic Energy
4. Roll for secondary chain (one event triggering another)
5. Maximum chain depth: 3

Each link in the chain generates Cosmic Energy. Deep chains are efficient Cosmic Energy generators.

## UI layout

All UI on CanvasLayer above the 3D scene.

- **Top bar**: Cosmic Dust (left, large, with notation), Cosmic Energy bar (centre, always visible, fills toward launch threshold), secondary Dust display (right)
- **Asteroid belt**: Arc beneath planet. Standard slots with cooldown arcs. Special weapon slots with type glow. Epoch asteroid slot — largest, always glowing when assembled, inert when not.
- **Coordinate crosshair**: Small persistent display showing target resolution — horizontal line (latitude), vertical line (longitude), locked crosshair when both confirmed. Updates as fragments are collected.
- **Fragment tracker**: Small arc showing Late age fragment progress — empty sockets filling as fragments are collected
- **Cursor**: Context-sensitive. Collection circle in space (size upgradeable). Scanner/magnifier on planet (reveals targets, click to tag).
- **Tag indicators**: Colour-coded rings on tagged tiles, visible as planet rotates
- **Hover panel**: Near cursor, tile hover only. Species picture, name, flavour text, population, Dust and Energy value if hit, tag info if tagged.
- **Extinction stamp**: Centre screen, 2D illustration, flavour text. Skippable.
- **Miss stamp**: Centre screen on Big Game window expiry. Master's voice. Skippable.
- **Animals in space**: 2D sprite tumbles across screen on qualifying events. Rare. No UI chrome.
- **Upgrade notification**: Peripheral, 2–3 seconds. Master's voice. One line.
- **Master voice lines**: Peripheral text, brief, at key moments. Never obstructs play.
- **Epoch title card**: Centre screen on transition. Epoch name, Master's subtitle. Fades after 3 seconds.
- **Big Game Hunter panel**: Persistent. Five targets with illustration. Current age highlighted, others greyed. Window timer per active target.
- **Post-age results**: Brief overlay, skippable. % exterminated, Dust gained, Cosmic Energy gained, chain reactions triggered.
- **Upgrade screen**: Full screen overlay between epochs. Prestige tree. Spend Cosmic Dust freely.
- **Statistics screen**: Accessible via button. Lifetime stats with Master's commentary.
